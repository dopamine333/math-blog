Definition. Subobject equivalence relation [0009]

  • October 31, 2026
  • Hsu Ching Chung

Two monomorphisms u, v into the same object A in a category \mathcal {C} are considered equivalent if u \le v \text { and } v \le u, denoted by u \sim v. Recall that u \le v denotes the subobject inclusion relation.

Context

    Definition of subobjects [000F]

    • November 2, 2026
    • Hsu Ching Chung

    For the generalization of a subset, we should look at a monomorphism i : U \hookrightarrow A. In the notation of generalized elements, the condition of i : U \hookrightarrow A being a monomorphism is exactly that, for all generalized elements x, y of U with the same context, if i x = i y then x = y. Notice that i x, i y are generalized elements of A. Therefore, for each object \Gamma , the post-composition by i: \begin {aligned} (i \circ ) : \operatorname {Hom}_\mathcal {C}(\Gamma , U) &\rightarrow \operatorname {Hom}_\mathcal {C}(\Gamma , A) \\ x &\mapsto i x \end {aligned} can be seen as an inclusion map from generalized elements of U to they of A.

    We can recover the notation of an element lying a subset, the inclusion relation between two subsets, and when two subsets are considered equal.

    Definition. Subobject membership relation [0007]

    • October 31, 2026
    • Hsu Ching Chung

    Let x : \Gamma \rightarrow A be a generalized element of an object A and u : U \hookrightarrow A be a monomorphism in a category \mathcal {C}. We say x is lying in u, denoted by x \in u, if x factors through u; that is, we have x = u \phi for some morphism \phi : \Gamma \rightarrow U.

    Notice that such a \phi is unique: if x = u \phi and x = u \psi for some \phi , \psi : \Gamma \rightarrow U, then u \phi = u \psi , which implies \phi = \psi since u is a monomorphism.

    Definition. Subobject inclusion relation [0008]

    • October 31, 2026
    • Hsu Ching Chung

    For monomorphisms u, v into the same object A in a category \mathcal {C}, we say u is included in v, denoted by u \le v, if x \in u \text { implies } x \in v for all generalized elements x of A. Recall that x \in u denotes the subobject membership relation.

    Definition. Subobject equivalence relation [0009]

    • October 31, 2026
    • Hsu Ching Chung

    Two monomorphisms u, v into the same object A in a category \mathcal {C} are considered equivalent if u \le v \text { and } v \le u, denoted by u \sim v. Recall that u \le v denotes the subobject inclusion relation.

    After we have the membership relation x \in u, all the other definitions u \le v, u \sim v mimic the behavier of a subset in set theory.

    Remarkably, in the above definition of u \le v, we hide the sources \Gamma , U, V of morphisms x, u, v intentionally. It is somehow irrelevant, and lets us write computations more like we are dealing with sets. In the following exercise, try to avoid explicitly writing the sources of morphisms as much as possible!

    We will see some examples of monomorphisms in the next section. First, let us make sure those definitions have the properties we expected and verify they match the definitions in nLab.

    Exercise. Properties of u \le v and u \sim v [000A]

    • October 31, 2026
    • Hsu Ching Chung

    For any monomorphisms u,u',v,v',w into A in a category \mathcal {C} and generalized element x of A, show the following:

    1. u \le v if and only if u factor through v.
    2. u \sim v if and only if u = v \phi for some isomorphism \phi : U \cong V, where U, V are the source of u : U \hookrightarrow A, v : V \hookrightarrow A respectively.
    3. The relation \le defines a preorder on monomorphisms into A:
      • u \le u.
      • If u \le v and v \le w then u \le w.
    4. The relation \sim defines a equivalence relation on monomorphisms into A:
      • u \sim u.
      • If u \sim v then v \sim u.
      • If u \sim v and v \sim w then u \sim w.
    5. Both relations x \in u and u \le v are well defined with respect to the equivalent relation u \sim v, that is:
      • If u \sim u' then x \in u if and only if x \in u'.
      • If u \sim u' and v \sim v' then u \le v if and only if u' \le v'.

    We should close this section by giving the definition of the subobjects of an object A.

    Definition. Subobject [000B]

    • November 2, 2026
    • Hsu Ching Chung

    The subobjects on an object A in a category \mathcal {C} are the equivalence classes of monomorphisms into A quotiented by the subobject equivalence relation \sim , denoted by \operatorname {Sub}(A). In notation: \operatorname {Sub}(A) := \set { u | u : U \hookrightarrow V }/{\sim }.

    As an abuse of notation, for two monomorphisms u,v into A, instead of writing [u] for the subobject represented by u and [u] = [v] for u, v are representing the same subobject, we just write u \in \operatorname {Sub}(A) and u \sim v.

        Definition. Monomorphism [0006]

        • October 31, 2026
        • Hsu Ching Chung

        A morphism f : A \rightarrow B in a category \mathcal {C} is called a monomorphism if f g = f h \text { implies } g = h for all morphisms g, h: C \rightarrow A.

        In this case, we also write f : A \hookrightarrow B or say f is monic.
