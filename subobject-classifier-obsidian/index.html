    I wrote a sketch of an introduction to the subobject classifier last Thursday. I was focusing on math and wrote any English that came to mind. It turned out to be filled with 渾然天成的 grammar errors. I have been rewriting the sketch as a polished blog and trying to host it on Github this week. For the choice of the blog tool, I tried Forester, a Zettelkasten system for math notes, and Kodama, a variation of Forester that allows Typst. A wise man once said: Stay away from LaTeX and use Typst to make your life better. But I still chose Forester for the bigger community and support (pain).

    I use the following steps to rewrite my English in the sketch:

    1. Pay attention to the grammar of the sentences and read them aloud. Try my best to fix them.
    2. Use the prompt point out the grammar errors but do not fix it with every section I have rewritten, and get 10+ more grammar errors.
    3. Be confused by the vocabulary about English grammar rules. Who knows the English of 被動分詞 is "Passive Participle"?
    4. Repeat the above steps until hours have passed and it's dinner time or the bus is coming and I need to go home.
    The result (I only had time to rewrite the first two chapters) looks good to me. I may need to tweak my method, otherwise I may not even read any synthetic differential geometry by the end of this semester. One of my friends also vibecoded a Forester-like Typst blog tool, and he converted my sketch to this in just 10 minutes...
